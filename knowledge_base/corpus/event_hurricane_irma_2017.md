# Historical Event: Hurricane Irma (September 2017)

Hurricane Irma tracked up the Florida peninsula in early-to-mid September 2017,
bringing heavy rainfall and river flooding across central and northern Florida,
including the St. Johns River basin. The St. Johns is a slow-flowing, low-gradient
river, so its flood response to heavy rainfall tends to be more gradual and
longer-lasting than a flashier system -- gauge height can remain elevated for
multiple weeks after the causative rainfall rather than receding within days. This
event is useful as a contrast case to Harvey's flashier bayou response and Sandy's
faster-receding tidal-river response, illustrating that "flood" classification must
account for basin-specific hydrologic response time, not just the size of the
discharge/stage change.

Classification reference: gauge height at St. Johns River sites should show a
gradual, sustained rise beginning within days of Irma's Florida landfall (Sep 10,
2017) and persisting for an extended recession period.
