# Historical Event: Hurricane Sandy (October 2012)

Hurricane Sandy made landfall near the New Jersey coast on October 29, 2012, as a
post-tropical cyclone, producing a destructive storm surge along the NY/NJ coastline
combined with heavy inland rainfall. Beyond the coastal storm surge, inland rivers in
New Jersey (including the Delaware, Passaic, and their tributaries such as Assunpink
Creek) saw discharge rise sharply in the days following landfall as rainfall drained
through their watersheds, producing riverine flooding distinct from, but concurrent
with, the coastal surge event. This is a useful case study for an agent because the
structured signal (river discharge/gauge height) rises with a multi-day lag relative
to the storm's landfall date, while social-media text reports of flooding/damage tend
to cluster tightly around the landfall date itself.

Classification reference: expect discharge at affected NJ river gauges to roughly
double relative to pre-storm baseline within 2-4 days of landfall (Oct 29), consistent
with NWS moderate/major riverine flood stage at multiple downstream gauges.
