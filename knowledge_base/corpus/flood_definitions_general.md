# WMO Definition of a Flood

The World Meteorological Organization defines a flood as "a rise, usually brief, in the
water level in a stream to a peak from which the water level recedes at a slower rate."
More broadly, floods are described as the temporary covering of normally dry land by
water outside its usual confines, arising from riverine overflow or intense local
rainfall exceeding drainage capacity (pluvial/flash flooding).

# Flood Types Relevant to This Benchmark

- **Flash flooding**: rapid-onset flooding, typically within a few hours of the causative
  rainfall, in small/steep watersheds or urban areas with limited drainage capacity.
  Indicated by a very sharp, short-duration spike in water level. This is the dominant
  flood type for Mineirinho Creek -- see `mineirinho_creek_criteria.md` for the specific
  site's classification criteria and physical setting.
- **Riverine flooding**: sustained or peak discharge exceeds the channel's capacity from
  prolonged upstream precipitation, producing a slower rise/recession than flash flooding.

Storm-surge/coastal flooding is not relevant to this benchmark -- Mineirinho Creek is an
inland urban canal with no tidal influence.
