# Historical Event: Hurricane Harvey (August 2017)

Hurricane Harvey made landfall on the Texas coast in late August 2017 and then stalled
over the Houston metropolitan area for several days, producing catastrophic,
record-breaking rainfall accumulations and widespread, severe flooding along Houston-area
bayous and rivers (including Buffalo Bayou and the San Jacinto River). Unlike a typical
fast-moving hurricane, Harvey's prolonged stall over one region is what made the flooding
so severe -- rainfall totals accumulated over days rather than hours. Structured gauge
data at Houston-area sites during this window should show a sharp, sustained rise in
discharge that remains elevated for an extended period (rather than a brief single-day
spike), reflecting the storm's slow movement. This event is widely represented in
crisis-response social media datasets (informativeness and humanitarian-category
labeled tweets), making it well suited for testing the joint structured+text agent.

Classification reference: sustained multi-day discharge elevation well above baseline
at Buffalo Bayou / San Jacinto River gauges during Aug 26 - Sep 2, 2017 is consistent
with NWS major flood stage.
